This week's Democracy Clock moved only one-tenth of a minute, but the stillness concealed a genuine contest of power. President Trump declared a crime emergency in Washington, D.C., seized command of the city's police, and floated making federal control permanent, before a legal challenge and political pressure forced a partial retreat. Alongside this, immigration enforcement widened into a broader system of detention and coercion, federal statistics faced direct political interference with the firing of the BLS commissioner, and public memory institutions came under pressure to conform to an official narrative. Texas advanced an aggressive new congressional map, spreading redistricting conflict to other states. Courts and local officials blunted several of these efforts, but the underlying structural claims on power largely survived the week's retreats.
