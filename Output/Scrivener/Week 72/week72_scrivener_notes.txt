Title
-----
The Clock Holds, Barely

Subtitle
--------
Force, money, and quiet rule changes met real judicial and congressional pushback, leaving the week's balance of power almost exactly where it began.

Tagline / Hook
-------------
When force meets resistance in equal measure, the clock doesn't fall or rise. It holds its breath.

Synopses
--------
Long Synopsis:
Week 72 (May 30–June 5, 2026) saw the Democracy Clock shift only two-tenths of a minute, from 7:42 to 7:41 p.m., as opposing forces largely canceled out. New Jersey State Police pepper-sprayed protesters and a sitting senator at Delaney Hall, while curfews and a 'free speech zone' restricted assembly near the facility. ICE quietly ended detainee-death reporting requirements as conditions worsened nationwide. Trump stripped civil service protections from thousands of federal workers and steered contracts toward Dell and a firm linked to his son. Courts pushed back forcefully: the Kennedy Center renaming was blocked, the transgender service ban was found likely unconstitutional, and birthright citizenship was upheld. Yet the same Supreme Court let Alabama's discriminatory map stand. Congress asserted itself on war powers while funding ICE without restraint. The contested Anti-Weaponization Fund was nominally dropped but not legally foreclosed.

Short Synopsis:
A week of canceling forces: state violence against protesters and a senator, a civil service purge, and insider enrichment were met by real judicial and congressional resistance. The clock barely moved, reflecting balance rather than calm.

Actionable Outcomes
-------------------

- Follow and support litigation challenging the civil service order stripping job protections from senior federal employees.
- Contact congressional representatives regarding oversight of ICE's rescinded detainee-death reporting requirement.
- Track and publicize the OMB proposal to politically vet federal research grants during its public comment period.
