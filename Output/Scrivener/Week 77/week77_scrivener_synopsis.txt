Week 77 was defined by coordinated pressure across elections, immigration, transparency, and public memory rather than a single constitutional break. The sharpest structural move came in election administration: Justice Department letters threatened state officials over voter-roll compliance, and the president fired the remaining Election Assistance Commission commissioners, weakening neutral election infrastructure while pushing restrictive voting measures as reform. Immigration enforcement intensified through mass ICE arrests, detention expansion, a fatal Houston shooting, and the detention of eyewitnesses, while court rulings narrowed review of Temporary Protected Status decisions and reinforced federal control over legal belonging. Across other domains, records were delayed or withheld, war imagery was reportedly suppressed, and public symbolism was bent toward leader-centered commemoration. Courts still imposed meaningful but scattered checks, protecting privacy, academic speech, and compliance with prior judgments.
