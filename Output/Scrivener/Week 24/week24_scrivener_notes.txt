Title
-----
The Flat Week That Wasn't

Subtitle
--------
A sweeping tax law, a detention buildout, and a narrowed judiciary moved power decisively even as the public clock barely ticked.

Tagline / Hook
-------------
When the clock barely moves, the damage often runs deepest beneath the surface, waiting to be counted later.

Synopses
--------
Long Synopsis:
This week the Democracy Clock held nearly still, moving six seconds, while underlying conditions shifted sharply. The Supreme Court limited nationwide injunctions against federal policy. The Senate declined to check unauthorized military action in Iran. Congress, under intense pressure, passed the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, permanently extending 2017 tax cuts while cutting Medicaid and SNAP and funding a massive expansion of ICE detention. Florida opened Alligator Alcatraz. Enforcement grew rougher: a Cuban man died in custody, a longtime resident was detained gardening, a photographer was arrested filming a raid. Courts blocked some actions, including TPS termination for Haitians. Large protests continued. The flat number concealed a week in which executive power grew, citizenship stratified, and resistance held only partially.

Short Synopsis:
A flat clock reading masked major movement: a sweeping tax-and-detention law, a Supreme Court ruling curbing judicial oversight, and rougher immigration enforcement, partially offset by court rulings and mass protest.
