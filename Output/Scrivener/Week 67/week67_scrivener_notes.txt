Metadata for Week 67

Title
-----
Maps, Monuments, and Leverage

Subtitle
--------
A voting-rights ruling, a president's building project, and selective legal pressure turned formal process into a method of democratic narrowing.

Tagline / Hook
-------------
Nothing dramatic had to happen for the system to worsen; this was a week when rulings, contracts, and guidance quietly taught power how to serve itself.

Synopses
--------
Long Synopsis:
Week 67 brought almost no visible movement on the public clock, but it deepened several authoritarian habits at once. The Supreme Court's Louisiana v. Callais ruling weakened a central Voting Rights Act protection, and state officials in Florida, Louisiana, Alabama, Mississippi, and Tennessee moved quickly to convert doctrine into partisan mapmaking and election disruption. At the same time, immigration enforcement expanded through family detention, new detention planning, speech-based green-card guidance, and large proposed funding increases without matching safeguards. The Justice Department appeared increasingly instrumental, from the James Comey indictment to pressure on litigation over Trump's White House ballroom. Media pressure also shifted from rhetoric to regulatory leverage through FCC action against ABC and Disney. The Democracy Clock held at 8:13 p.m., moving only 0.1 minutes, a near-stall that reflected consolidation rather than relief.

Short Synopsis:
The week's central movement was conversion: court rulings, prosecutions, budgets, and security fears were turned into tools for narrowing representation, oversight, and neutrality without breaking formal procedure.
