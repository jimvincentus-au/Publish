Metadata for Week 15

Title
-----
Widening the Base of Erosion

Subtitle
--------
A week of small, repeated acts across immigration, courts, media, and universities revealed a government rewarding loyalty and narrowing accountability.

Tagline / Hook
-------------
When ordinary governance and extraordinary power grabs start looking the same, stillness on the clock is not peace but momentum.

Synopses
--------
Long Synopsis:
During Week 15, the Democracy Clock held nearly still, moving just 0.3 minutes, even as democratic erosion widened across nearly every domain of governance. Immigration enforcement reached into homes, schools, and mailboxes, deporting citizen children and detaining lawful residents. Courts remained a genuine brake, blocking the Alien Enemies Act and restoring funding, even as a sitting judge was arrested and officials called for more arrests. Universities faced pressure from Washington and turned on their own protesters. Independent journalism lost access and funding while a White House-run outlet rose in its place. Access to power became explicit: a half-million-dollar club, a memecoin dinner, and eighty-nine paused corporate investigations. Congress mostly declined to resist. The clock's stillness reflects not calm but a steadiness of erosion no longer requiring acceleration.

Short Synopsis:
Week 15 saw erosion spread rather than sharpen: immigration enforcement into homes and mail, judges arrested and attacked, universities squeezed from both sides, media starved while loyal outlets rose, and access to power openly sold, even as courts kept offering real resistance.
