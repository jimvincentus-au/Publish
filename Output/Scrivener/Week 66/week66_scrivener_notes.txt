Title

Pressure Spread Across Institutions

Subtitle
--------
Immigration, law enforcement, elections, and public information all came under coordinated pressure, while courts and lawmakers offered only partial checks.

Tagline / Hook
-------------
The week did not break democracy in one place; it pulled more institutions into the same pattern of pressure, exposure, and conditional protection.

Synopses
--------
Long Synopsis:
Week 66 brought no single constitutional rupture, but a wider spread of coercive politics across public life. Immigration enforcement expanded from raids and detention into status insecurity itself, with more judges fired, DACA protections narrowed, and denaturalization plans advanced. Legal pressure also moved outward toward critics: the Southern Poverty Law Center was indicted, a New York Times reporter was investigated, and FBI Director Kash Patel sued The Atlantic. Election administration became more adversarial as voter-data demands, citizenship challenges, and litigation made ordinary registration newly contestable. At the same time, war policy toward Iran carried domestic costs and conflict-of-interest scrutiny, while donor money, family-linked ventures, and regulatory discretion repeatedly appeared in the same frame. Courts and some lawmakers still forced disclosures, blocked some actions, and opened inquiries, but mostly after executive pressure had already widened. The clock held because resistance remained active, not because the week was stable.

Short Synopsis:
Week 66 widened the field of democratic pressure. Immigration status, legal retaliation, voter data, public information, and donor influence all came under strain, while courts and Congress managed only partial, reactive resistance.
