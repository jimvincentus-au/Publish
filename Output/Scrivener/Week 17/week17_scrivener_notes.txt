Title

Stillness That Masks Motion

Subtitle
--------
Courts brake executive overreach while foreign self-dealing, retaliation, and information control advance largely unchecked beneath a flat clock reading.

Tagline / Hook
-------------
When the clock doesn't move, look closer: this week's stillness concealed a government testing due process, monetizing foreign favor, and punishing inconvenient truths.

Synopses
--------
Long Synopsis:
In Week 17, the Democracy Clock held at 7:40 p.m., a net change of zero minutes masking intense internal movement. Federal courts repeatedly blocked deportations, freed detained activists, and forced due-process limits on the Alien Enemies Act, even as the administration expanded enforcement, questioned due process outright, and floated suspending habeas corpus. Newark's mayor was arrested at a private detention facility protest. The administration confirmed plans to accept a $400 million Qatari jet amid broader Gulf dealmaking, prompting a Senate hold on DOJ nominees. Officials who testified inconveniently were fired; loyalists were elevated. The White House launched state-aligned media while VOA lost hundreds of jobs. A reconciliation bill carrying deep social cuts stalled in committee. Courts held a line; nearly everything else pressed forward.

Short Synopsis:
The clock stayed flat, but beneath it courts fought executive overreach on immigration and due process while foreign self-dealing, retaliatory firings, and information control advanced. Congress produced a rare institutional check by stalling a sweeping reconciliation bill.
