Week 76 Appendix: Executive Alignment Without Net Movement
This was a structurally consequential week defined by three overlapping patterns: judicial expansion of presidential control, intensified use of state power against immigrants and dissent, and increasingly open fusion of public office with private enrichment and symbolic self-glorification. The single biggest shift came from the Supreme Court’s Slaughter ruling and related reactions, which weakened independent agencies and materially strengthened the unitary-executive model, even as a narrower Fed ruling and several voting-rights and birthright-citizenship decisions imposed partial limits elsewhere. At the same time, the administration and aligned actors pressed restrictive election measures, escalated ICE arrests, pursued protest-related prosecutions, and expanded opaque surveillance practices through federal websites. Parallel to this, multiple disclosures and reports pointed to a governing style in which public resources, foreign relationships, and regulatory choices are entangled with Trump-family financial interests, from crypto and Kazakhstan to no-bid contracts and anniversary branding. The week therefore shows not a single rupture but a coordinated pressure pattern: centralization of executive authority, selective legal coercion, and normalization of corruption-adjacent governance under patriotic and administrative cover.
