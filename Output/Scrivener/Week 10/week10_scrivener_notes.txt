Title
-----
A Government Learns Its Own Strength

Subtitle
--------
Law firms, judges, universities, and federal workers face coordinated pressure while the clock barely moves, hiding a week of broad erosion.

Tagline / Hook
-------------
The clock stood still while everything beneath it moved—law, courts, memory, and labor, tested together and found wanting.

Synopses
--------
Long Synopsis:
Week 10 tested multiple democratic institutions simultaneously rather than one at a time. Executive orders punished law firms for representing the president's opponents, splitting targets between settlement and litigation. Judges faced impeachment calls, mockery, and defiance of deportation orders even as the Chief Justice publicly rejected court-packing threats. Immigration enforcement targeted students for political speech, while universities capitulated to funding threats. An executive order stripped collective bargaining from over a million federal workers, paired with agency dismantlement at HHS, FEMA, and USAID. A Signal chat scandal exposed reckless handling of classified war plans, followed by denial and attacks on the journalist who revealed it. A new voting-rights order and a Smithsonian directive extended executive reach into elections and historical memory. The public clock held steady at 7:33 p.m., but nearly every tracked trait moved toward erosion beneath that stillness.

Short Synopsis:
A week of accumulation rather than rupture: law firms, courts, universities, and federal workers all came under pressure at once. The public clock barely moved, but almost every underlying democratic trait shifted toward erosion, revealing consolidation disguised as calm.

Actionable Outcomes
-------------------
- Follow and support the pending lawsuits filed by Jenner & Block and WilmerHale challenging retaliatory executive orders against law firms.
- Contact congressional representatives regarding proposed legislation to strip federal courts of nationwide injunctive power.
- Monitor and engage with legal challenges to the voter-registration executive order, particularly its documentary proof-of-citizenship requirement.
- Support organizations providing legal defense for detained students facing removal proceedings tied to protected political speech.
- Attend or organize lawful public rallies and town halls to sustain visible civic engagement on these issues.
- Track disclosures related to the Signal chat records-preservation lawsuit filed by American Oversight.
- Contact state attorneys general to encourage scrutiny of campaign spending practices in judicial elections.
