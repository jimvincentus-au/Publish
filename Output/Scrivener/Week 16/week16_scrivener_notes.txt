Title
-----
A Week of Settling, Not Rupture

Subtitle
--------
Coercion becomes routine across media, universities, and immigration enforcement while courts provide narrow, partial checks on executive power.

Tagline / Hook
-------------
When abuse becomes routine, even a week full of coercion can register as confirmation rather than escalation—until the pattern itself becomes the danger.

Synopses
--------
Long Synopsis:
The Democracy Clock barely moved this week, advancing only two-tenths of a minute to 7:40:12 p.m., because so much of what occurred repeated established patterns rather than breaking new ground. Voice of America stayed dark while its leadership contracted with a partisan outlet to fill the silence; the FCC investigated CBS even as its parent sought merger approval. Harvard lost federal grants over ideological demands, and police cleared protest encampments at several universities while Michigan dropped charges against others. Newark's mayor was arrested protesting a detention facility, and officials floated suspending habeas corpus. Courts freed several detainees, protected an election result in North Carolina, and partially checked the media shutdown. The federal workforce was reshaped around loyalty as watchdogs were sidelined. Neither executive pressure nor judicial resistance won decisively; both continued as before.

Short Synopsis:
A week dense with coercive activity but light on new structural damage: media pressure, university funding threats, immigration intimidation, and civil service purges all continued familiar patterns. Courts pushed back unevenly, freeing detainees and protecting an election outcome, but the clock barely moved.
