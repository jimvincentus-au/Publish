Week 64 saw the clock hold nearly still at 7:43 p.m., a stillness masking one of the term's gravest ruptures. The president threatened Iran with civilizational destruction, then unilaterally declared a two-week ceasefire without congressional input, floating a personal financial venture with the same adversary he had just threatened. Cabinet officials offered no resistance. House Republicans twice blocked war-powers votes through procedural refusal, even as more than eighty-five Democrats and some Republicans invoked impeachment and the 25th Amendment. Courts continued ruling against executive overreach while judges faced coordinated intimidation. A federal voter-eligibility purge advanced using an error-prone database. Fifty-three cities canceled surveillance contracts after grassroots pressure, the week's clearest proof that resistance outside Washington still works. Escalation and restraint held each other in a standoff, not an equilibrium.
