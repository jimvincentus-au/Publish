Title
-----
Patronage, Voting, and Public Myth

Subtitle
--------
Executive protection, weaker voting safeguards, and leader-centered commemoration advanced together while courts and lawmakers offered only partial resistance.

Tagline / Hook
-------------
Not a week of rupture, but of arrangement: law, money, elections, and memory were sorted to serve power more efficiently.

Synopses
--------
Long Synopsis:
Week 70 brought a small but meaningful move forward on the Democracy Clock as executive power was arranged to protect allies, shape elections, and claim public symbolism. The Justice Department created a $1.776 billion Anti-Weaponization Fund while extending tax-related protection to Trump, his family, and affiliated companies, joining public money to legal immunity. The Supreme Court weakened Voting Rights Act protections, and state officials in South Carolina and North Carolina moved quickly on redistricting and election rules that critics said would dilute or suppress Black voting power. The administration also redirected America250 planning into Freedom 250 and staged Christian nationalist public ceremony while advancing leader-centered monuments and donor access. Courts, some lawmakers, and civil society pushed back, especially on records, immigration tactics, and oversight. But most resistance was reactive, narrow, and slower than the structural changes underway.

Short Synopsis:
Week 70 moved the clock slightly forward as the administration fused legal protection, public money, voting-rights rollback, and symbolic glorification into a broader pattern of personal rule. Courts and some lawmakers resisted, but mostly after the ground had already shifted.
