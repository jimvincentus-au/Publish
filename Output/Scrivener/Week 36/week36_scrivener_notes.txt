Title
-----
A Week Fluent in Retaliation

Subtitle
--------
Prosecutorial pressure, media coercion, and enforcement expansion moved faster this week, meeting less resistance than before.

Tagline / Hook
-------------
Not a single rupture, but a fluency: the machinery of retribution moved faster this week, and met less resistance than it has before.

Synopses
--------
Long Synopsis:
The Democracy Clock held nearly still this week, moving from 7:42 p.m. to 7:41 p.m., as genuine institutional resistance offset a heavier accumulation of executive overreach. The clearest thread ran through the indictment of James Comey: after the president demanded prosecutions of named opponents, a career prosecutor who found insufficient evidence against Letitia James resigned under pressure, replaced by a loyalist who secured an indictment within days. Alongside this, an executive order designated Antifa a domestic terrorist organization and a new memorandum authorized sweeping investigation of activists and nonprofits. Media pressure intensified as regulators pushed a network to suspend Jimmy Kimmel, while affiliate owners kept his show dark even after reinstatement. Immigration enforcement expanded as judicial oversight was dismantled, and insider enrichment surfaced in an Argentina currency swap benefiting a Treasury official's associate. Courts, Senate Democrats, and journalists offered real resistance, but it arrived after policies were substantially executed.

Short Synopsis:
Federal power was used this week to reward loyalty and punish dissent, from Comey's indictment to expanded terrorism designations and media coercion. Courts and Congress pushed back in places, but resistance mostly followed rather than prevented the damage, leaving the clock nearly unchanged.
