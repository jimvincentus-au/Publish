Title
-----
The Week the Clock Held Still

Subtitle
--------
A tariff ruling was defied within days, immigration enforcement widened, and records fights deepened, yet the measured week produced no net movement.

Tagline / Hook
-------------
A court ruling, defied within days. A refugee, abandoned to die. A Congress, admitting its own irrelevance. The clock did not move, but nothing stood still.

Synopses
--------
Long Synopsis:
In Week 58, the Supreme Court struck down President Trump's tariff regime, only for him to reimpose global tariffs under new legal authority within days, while Congress admitted it lacked the votes to codify or block his actions. Immigration enforcement expanded sharply, touching a five-year-old asylum seeker, a Columbia student, and a nearly blind refugee who was later found dead after an unnotified release. At the State of the Union, a silent guest was arrested and a congressman ejected. Records fights over the Jack Smith report and Epstein files left transparency in permanent doubt. Courts, states, and local communities pushed back in scattered places. These currents mostly canceled out: the clock remained at 7:43 p.m., unchanged from the prior week, reflecting consolidation rather than either collapse or repair.

Short Synopsis:
The Supreme Court struck down Trump's tariffs; he reimposed them days later under new authority. Immigration enforcement widened, records fights deepened, and dissent was met with arrest. Despite substantial negative movement, the clock held at 7:43 p.m.
