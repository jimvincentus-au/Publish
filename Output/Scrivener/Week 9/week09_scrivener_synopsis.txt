The week opened with the invocation of the 1798 Alien Enemies Act to deport hundreds of Venezuelan and Salvadoran men without hearings, and closed with Chief Justice Roberts publicly defending a judge under attack. Between those points, deportation flights proceeded despite a judge's restraining order, a Maryland resident with protected status was wrongly sent to a Salvadoran prison, and immigration authority was turned against student activists and academics. The administration dismantled the Department of Education and Voice of America by executive order, coerced a major law firm into concessions, and pressed universities with funding cuts. Federal judges pushed back repeatedly—on USAID, Social Security data access, and a transgender military ban—while Congress mostly amplified executive action rather than checking it. The clock barely moved, reflecting a week where erosion and resistance ran nearly even.
