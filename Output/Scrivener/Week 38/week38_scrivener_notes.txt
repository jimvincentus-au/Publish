Synopses
--------
Long Synopsis:
In a week the clock moved just six seconds, the administration deployed National Guard troops to Chicago and Portland over gubernatorial objection, then rerouted troops from other states after judges blocked the moves outright. Senior officials discussed invoking the Insurrection Act while claiming plenary domestic military authority. Federal agents used tear gas and pepper spray against protesters, journalists, and a bystander shot during pursuit. Career prosecutors who declined politically directed charges were fired and replaced by loyalists who indicted Letitia James and prosecuted James Comey. Congress delayed a newly elected representative's swearing-in to block an Epstein files vote. Courts held, a career prosecutor resisted, and one Republican governor broke ranks—narrow points of resistance against a system testing how far it could push without technically breaking.

Short Synopsis:
Troop deployments defied court orders through jurisdictional workarounds, prosecutors were purged for resisting political pressure, and dissent was branded as terrorism—while courts, one prosecutor, and one governor held the line, keeping the clock's movement modest but its direction unmistakable.
