Week 63 left the Democracy Clock unmoved at 8:13 p.m., but the stillness masked a week of structural consolidation. Trump issued sweeping executive orders to build federal citizenship lists and condition mail voting and election funding on state compliance, while Florida and Mississippi added proof‑of‑citizenship barriers. The Justice Department sued for unredacted voter rolls and pressed a radical challenge to birthright citizenship at the Supreme Court, even as abusive detention practices and third‑country deportations hardened a stratified regime of membership. Abroad, the Iran war justified emergency spending workarounds, record defense budgets, and threats against civilian infrastructure, with a private billionaire folded into strategy calls and NATO commitments treated as optional. At home, loyalty purges at Justice and in the Army, an opinion attacking the Presidential Records Act, and a campaign to sanitize museums and historic sites showed law, archives, and memory being bent toward regime needs. Yet millions marched in No Kings protests, unions planned May Day actions, and courts, in scattered rulings, defended mail voting, public media, protest rights, and honest history. The balance between erosion and resistance held, but the architecture of power grew clearer and more entrenched.
