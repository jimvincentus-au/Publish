In the opening week of Donald Trump's second term, the Democracy Clock moved from 7:31 to 7:32 p.m., a small shift measuring an enormous burst of activity. Dozens of executive orders, two national emergencies, a mass pardon of January 6 defendants, a purge of inspectors general, and an attempt to end birthright citizenship all landed within five days. Courts pushed back once, blocking the citizenship order as unconstitutional, but elsewhere speed outran oversight: watchdogs were fired before Congress could object, asylum pathways closed before advocates could sue, and civil service protections vanished before unions could organize. Pardons for political allies contrasted with investigations aimed at critics. The week reveals a governing method built on volume and velocity rather than any single decisive act.
