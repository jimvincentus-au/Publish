Title

Pressure Without Breakthrough

Subtitle
--------
Executive pressure widened across elections, dissent, intelligence, immigration, and war powers, while courts and Congress resisted unevenly enough to hold the line.

Tagline / Hook
-------------
A week of democratic strain in which executive pressure spread across institutions, and resistance arrived often enough to prevent a measurable break.

Synopses
--------
Long Synopsis:
Week 75 held the Democracy Clock at 8:14 p.m., but the stillness reflected collision, not calm. The administration broadened its push for control over election rules through funding threats, voter-roll access demands, proof-of-citizenship requirements, and mail-voting restrictions. Federal courts repeatedly blocked or slowed those efforts, preserving state authority and legal limits. At the same time, dissent was more openly cast as a security threat: antifa was designated a domestic terrorist organization, anti-ICE protesters faced terrorism-framed prosecutions, and public space around the Lincoln Memorial became fenced and policed. Immigration showed the sharpest institutional split, with lower courts protecting speech and due process even as the Supreme Court widened executive discretion over TPS, asylum, and deportation. Congress asserted itself on housing and war powers, then faltered under executive leverage. The week measured strain without net movement.

Short Synopsis:
The clock did not move, but pressure intensified. The administration pushed harder on elections, protest, intelligence, immigration, and Iran, while courts blocked several key moves and Congress resisted only unevenly.
