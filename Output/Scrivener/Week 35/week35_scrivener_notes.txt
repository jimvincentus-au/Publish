Synopses
--------
Long Synopsis:
Week 35 unfolded in the aftermath of Charlie Kirk's assassination, which the administration rapidly converted into justification for expanded coercive authority. Within days, rhetoric blaming the "radical left" became an executive memo, a domestic terrorism designation for Antifa, and firings of teachers, professors, and journalists nationwide. The FCC threatened ABC's broadcast license over a comedian's commentary, forcing Jimmy Kimmel's suspension before public backlash reinstated him. Trump ordered National Guard deployments to multiple Democratic-led cities without gubernatorial consent, while ICE enforcement grew harsher, including a fatal shooting and a raid that exceeded its warrant. Yet resistance held: a judge dismissed Trump's $15 billion lawsuit against the New York Times, a prosecutor resigned rather than indict named critics, and courts blocked the removal of a Federal Reserve governor. The Democracy Clock remained unchanged at 7:42 p.m., reflecting forces in near-perfect, precarious balance.

Short Synopsis:
A political assassination became raw material for expanded state power this week—regulatory threats, prosecutorial pressure, and military deployments moved almost simultaneously. Courts, a resignation, and public backlash pushed back hard enough that the week's net movement was zero, though the underlying leverage remains intact.
