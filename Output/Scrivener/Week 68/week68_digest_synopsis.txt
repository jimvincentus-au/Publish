This week opened with the Supreme Court's decision in Callais v. Louisiana, which effectively neutered Section 2 of the Voting Rights Act by requiring proof of discriminatory intent rather than effect. The consequences were immediate and nationwide: Louisiana suspended an active primary election, Tennessee erased its only majority-Black congressional district within 72 hours, and Alabama moved to reinstate a map already found racially gerrymandered. Courts pushed back unevenly—some blocked administration overreach on grants, sanctions, and detention policy, while Virginia's top court struck down a voter-approved map favoring Democrats.
