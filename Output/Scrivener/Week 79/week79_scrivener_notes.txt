Title
-----
Consolidation Without Clock Movement

Subtitle
--------
Voting pressure, harsher immigration enforcement, war opacity, and retaliation against scrutiny deepened existing democratic strain without moving the clock.

Tagline / Hook
-------------
No single rupture defined the week; democratic strain deepened through accumulation, as pressure spread across elections, immigration, war powers, and the institutions meant to watch them.

Synopses
--------
Long Synopsis:
Week 79 brought no net movement on the Democracy Clock, which remained at 7:44 p.m., but the stillness reflected consolidation rather than relief. The administration pressed voting restrictions through executive threats, grant conditions, and congressional packaging, while courts weakened key protections and allowed contested maps to stand. Immigration enforcement expanded in scale and severity, with record ICE arrests, deaths tied to operations, cuts to legal aid, and signs of weak internal accountability. War with Iran widened executive claims of urgency as Congress faced large funding requests, passed mixed signals on war powers, and struggled to obtain a stable public record of casualties and costs. Journalists and investigators faced retaliatory pressure, while donor influence and patronage concerns shadowed enforcement and defense policy. States, courts, and civic groups did resist in meaningful but fragmented ways.

Short Synopsis:
The clock held at 7:44 p.m., but the week hardened existing lines: voting restrictions advanced, immigration enforcement intensified, war oversight weakened, and scrutiny drew retaliation. Courts, states, and civic groups pushed back, yet the dominant pattern was reinforcement of already dangerous trends.
