A Week That Held Its Ground

--------

Tagline / Hook

Synopses
--------
Long Synopsis:
In Week 47, the Democracy Clock held steady at 7:43 p.m., its net movement zero. That stillness masked intense activity. Immigration enforcement hardened into permanent infrastructure through nationality-based restrictions, a new deportation aircraft fleet, and aggressive ICE actions reaching veterans, asylum seekers, and even a political commentator. President Trump pardoned an indicted ally and pressured rivals through selective prosecution, while a grand jury twice refused to indict Attorney General Letitia James and a judge blocked a National Guard deployment in Los Angeles. Indiana's legislature rejected federal pressure to redraw its congressional map, even as the Supreme Court let Texas keep a contested one. Technology policy, media ownership, and public-health regulation all bent toward private and political interests. The week's expansions and resistances canceled out almost exactly, leaving the balance unmoved but under visible strain.

Short Synopsis:
The clock did not move this week, but not for lack of trying. Immigration enforcement expanded into lasting infrastructure, retaliatory prosecutions multiplied, and executive reach grew into AI policy and media—while courts, a grand jury, and Indiana's legislature held firm against it.
