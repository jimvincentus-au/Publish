Synopses
--------
Long Synopsis:
Week 64 unfolded as an Iran war crisis fused with domestic institutional erosion. Trump renamed the Pentagon the Department of War, fired senior generals, threatened annihilation and NATO withdrawal, and cycled through fragile ceasefires, while Congress recessed, blocked war-powers votes, and routed $350 billion in defense spending—anchored by the Golden Dome missile shield—to reconciliation. Major contracts and an AI deal with xAI steered public money toward Trump-aligned firms, even as courts deferred to wartime discretion. At home, an executive order weaponized the flawed SAVE immigration database for voter eligibility, and health and immigration data were quietly linked to enforcement and election policing, prompting FOIA suits. Immigration enforcement and Florida’s anti-Sharia law hardened tiered citizenship. Simultaneously, an OLC opinion attacked the Presidential Records Act, DOJ shielded Epstein records, and Trump threatened journalists and whistleblowers. Yet cities canceled Flock surveillance, Wisconsin voters strengthened a pro-democracy court, and organizers planned a general strike, signaling ongoing but outmatched resistance.

Short Synopsis:
Iran war brinkmanship, SAVE-based voter policing, and Golden Dome patronage entrenched executive and crony power while archives, courts, and surveillance rules bent toward insiders. Courts, cities, and organizers pushed back, but the Democracy Clock’s public time stayed fixed.

Actionable Outcomes
-------------------
- Support and amplify litigation and watchdog efforts that challenge SAVE-based voter purges, FOIA stonewalling, and attacks on the Presidential Records Act.
- Help voters in vulnerable communities verify registration status early and often, especially where immigration or data-matching tools are being tied to election administration.
- Press local governments, school boards, and city councils to audit and, where necessary, cancel surveillance technologies like ALPR systems that can be used to track protests and communities.
- Back state and local candidates, including judicial races, who commit to protecting voting rights, independent courts, and nonpartisan administration of public agencies.
- Engage with public comment processes at agencies like EPA, CDC, FDA, and FCC to contest deregulatory or politicized rule changes and to defend transparent data practices.
