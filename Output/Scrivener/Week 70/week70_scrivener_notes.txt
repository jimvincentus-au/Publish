Title
-----
The Fund That Would Not Yield

Subtitle
--------
A settlement built a $1.776 billion executive fund and permanent tax immunity, testing whether resistance can catch harm before it hardens into law.

Tagline / Hook
-------------
A billion-dollar fund, a permanent shield from audit, and the fastest bipartisan revolt of the term — all in one week that moved nothing and changed everything.

Synopses
--------
Long Synopsis:
Week 70 began with a settlement: Trump dropped his ten-billion-dollar IRS suit in exchange for a $1.776 billion taxpayer fund and a permanent bar on IRS scrutiny of himself and his family. No statute authorized either. January 6 defendants applied for payouts within days. A federal judge found no valid settlement existed. Treasury's general counsel resigned. Roughly two dozen Republican senators confronted the acting attorney general and called the arrangement morally wrong, while House leadership avoided a bipartisan war-powers vote it feared losing. The Supreme Court narrowed Voting Rights Act enforcement; South Carolina moved against a Black-majority district within days. Courts dismissed vindictive prosecutions and blocked courthouse arrests. Border enforcement produced documented deaths. The clock did not move. Institutional damage and institutional resistance arrived in nearly matched proportion, both real, neither resolved.

Short Synopsis:
A settlement converted a presidential lawsuit into a $1.776 billion fund and permanent tax immunity. Bipartisan revolt, judicial pushback, and a Treasury resignation followed swiftly, but did not reverse it. The week's harm and resistance arrived in balance, leaving the clock unmoved.
