Week 77 Appendix: Election Pressure Without Rupture
This was a high-pressure week for democratic structure, defined less by a single constitutional rupture than by simultaneous assaults on election administration, immigrant rights, transparency, and the boundary between state power and personal or partisan retaliation. The most consequential development was the effective decapitation of the Election Assistance Commission alongside Justice Department threats to state election officials, which together increased pressure on traits involving unchecked executive power, voter suppression, and weaponized federal authority. A second major theme was coercive immigration enforcement: mass ICE arrests, new detention capacity, the fatal Houston shooting, detention of eyewitnesses, and court rulings narrowing review of TPS decisions all deepened stratified citizenship and weakened due process. Information integrity also deteriorated through opaque government conduct, pressure on private information channels, and media conflicts of interest. Countervailing judicial actions did appear—courts blocked some retaliatory or censorial actions, protected transgender medical privacy, and struck down part of Florida’s Stop Woke Act—but these were mostly defensive checks against a broader pattern of executive overreach and institutional politicization.
