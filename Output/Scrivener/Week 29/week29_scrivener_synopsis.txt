Week 29 brought no single rupture but a dense continuation of familiar patterns. Trump fired the BLS commissioner after weak jobs data, pressured the Federal Reserve chair, and imposed sweeping tariffs, treating economic institutions as instruments to align rather than sources of truth. The Justice Department opened retaliatory investigations while confirming Jeanine Pirro as a partisan U.S. attorney. Texas Democrats fled the state to block a redistricting map, and Governor Abbott answered with arrest orders and fines. Immigration enforcement expanded through detention contracts, local police deputization, and a proposed census excluding undocumented residents. Universities faced funding coercion, with Harvard choosing litigation while others settled. Courts, protests, and lawsuits offered partial resistance. The Democracy Clock held at 7:41 p.m., not from calm but because harms matched rather than exceeded the damage already embedded.
