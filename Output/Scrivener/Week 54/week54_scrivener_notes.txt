Title

Minneapolis Absorbs a Nation's Pressure

Subtitle
--------
A week of raids, a killing, and courtroom battles moved the clock only slightly while nearly everything moved at once.

Tagline / Hook
-------------
When force and resistance both peak at once, the clock records stillness—but stillness is not the same as peace.

Synopses
--------
Long Synopsis:
Federal agents detained a five-year-old boy and his father, then swept up two more children from his school days later. These acts were part of a broader Minneapolis campaign of raids reaching churches, businesses, and immigrant neighborhoods. The killing of Alex Pretti by federal agents became a second flashpoint, drawing bipartisan calls for investigation, a state agency's own rebuttal website, and a presidential dispatch of his border czar. Courts intervened repeatedly, ordering releases and preserving evidence, while Congress used funding leverage over Homeland Security. Meanwhile, the EPA rescinded its climate finding and suspended dissenting staff, and election administration in Minnesota and Georgia absorbed new federal pressure. The Democracy Clock barely moved, reflecting intense, canceling pressures from multiple directions rather than a single decisive shift.

Short Synopsis:
Minneapolis became the center of a week defined by child detentions, a fatal shooting by federal agents, and fierce institutional pushback. Courts, Congress, and citizens pressed back hard against expanding federal power, holding the needle nearly still even as intensity surged on every front.
