Title

The Week Power Accumulated Quietly

Subtitle
--------
Executive discretion widened, enforcement hardened, and dissent grew costlier while a hollowed disaster response killed dozens in Texas.

Tagline / Hook
-------------
Not a single seizure of power, but its patient, procedural accumulation—one contract delay, one purge, one tariff threat at a time.

Synopses
--------
Long Synopsis:
Across nine linked developments, the Democracy Clock recorded a week defined by accumulation rather than rupture. Flash floods in Texas killed dozens after FEMA contract lapses, a personal-approval bottleneck at DHS, and earlier weather-service staffing cuts weakened the response; officials then blamed the press rather than accounting for the failure. Meanwhile, the Supreme Court narrowed nationwide injunctions as Trump blocked education funds, limited classified sharing with Congress, struck Iran without authorization, and set tariffs by emergency decree. Congress ratified a budget law entrenching tax cuts while funding a militarized immigration crackdown. Loyalty purges reshaped national security, health, and diplomatic agencies. Courts offered narrow resistance on birthright citizenship and Florida's immigration law. The public clock barely moved, masking widely distributed institutional erosion.

Short Synopsis:
A week without a single dramatic rupture instead saw executive discretion widen, enforcement harden, and dissent grow costlier across courts, Congress, and agencies—while a hollowed disaster response in Texas showed what erosion costs in human lives.
