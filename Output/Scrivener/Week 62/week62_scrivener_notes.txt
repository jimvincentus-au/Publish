Metadata for Week 62

Title
-----
Emergency As Standing Policy

Subtitle
--------
War in Iran, a weaponized shutdown at home, and a coordinated squeeze on voting and citizenship turned crisis into the default mode of governance.

Tagline / Hook
-------------
Crisis stopped being an exception and became the method, as war, shutdown, and voter rules were all bent to consolidate executive power.

Synopses
--------
Long Synopsis:
Week 62 of Trump’s second term registered no net movement on the Democracy Clock, holding at 8:13 p.m., but only because resistance absorbed a surge of strain. Abroad, the Iran war hardened into an executive project: threats to “obliterate” power plants, large new deployments, sanctions maneuvers, and even acknowledgment of an unauthorized “military operation,” all shaped by lobbying from Saudi and Israeli leaders. At home, a prolonged DHS shutdown became leverage for immigration and voting demands, with ICE agents in tactical gear screening passengers and an executive order improvising pay for TSA workers. Parallel efforts sought to narrow and centralize voting through the SAVE America Act, litigation over mail ballots, ballot seizures, and a draft order to end machine and mail voting. Immigration enforcement intensified, courts and states pushed back case by case, and law and security institutions bent further toward partisan loyalty. Meanwhile, money and foreign investment threaded through health, energy, and Middle East policy, and Trump personalized national symbols and memory, even as “No Kings” protests and targeted lawsuits signaled a fragile but real civic countercurrent.

Short Synopsis:
The clock stayed at 8:13 p.m. as unilateral war-making, shutdown governance, voter restriction, and stratified citizenship advanced, offset by courts, states, and mass civic resistance straining to hold the line.
