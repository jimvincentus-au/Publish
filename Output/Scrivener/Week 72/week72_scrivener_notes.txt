Title

Administrative Hardening Without Advance

Subtitle

Tagline / Hook
-------------
The week did not break the system in one stroke; it taught public institutions to bend further toward loyalty, secrecy, and selective reward.

Synopses
--------
Long Synopsis:
Week 72 showed democratic erosion through administration rather than spectacle. Immigration detention became the clearest human story: detainees at Delaney Hall struck over conditions and legal access, reports documented suicides and contaminated food in custody, and oversight narrowed as ICE restricted congressional visits and ended one death-reporting channel. At the same time, the administration expanded coercive immigration power through an asset-seizure order and a Senate enforcement package that enlarged detention and border capacity. Elsewhere, the White House moved to politicize the civil service and public grants, while donor-linked contracts, loans, and prestige projects reinforced a pay-to-play pattern. The Justice Department added to rule-of-law concerns through retaliatory-looking investigations and equivocation about obeying court orders. Courts, cities, and some lawmakers pushed back in meaningful but uneven ways, offsetting the week’s damage without reversing its deeper pattern.

Short Synopsis:
Week 72 brought no clock movement, but not because conditions stabilized. Detention abuse, politicized staffing, weakened oversight, and donor-linked favoritism advanced, while courts, cities, and Congress supplied enough fragmented resistance to keep the week in near balance.
