Title
-----
The Week Erosion and Resistance Tied

Subtitle
--------
Federal force against a senator, a blocked slush fund, and a president's finances entwined with contracts defined a week of near-perfect balance.

Tagline / Hook
-------------
When force meets a hunger strike and courts meet a slush fund, the balance of a democracy is measured in tenths of a minute.

Synopses
--------
Long Synopsis:
Week 71 saw federal agents deploy pepper spray and batons across several days against detainees, protesters, journalists, and eventually a sitting U.S. senator outside a New Jersey detention facility, while DHS denied documented conditions. The president purged Republican dissenters through primaries, and an Iran nuclear deal collapsed within hours of its announcement. Self-dealing spread through federal contracting, from a Dell stock purchase preceding a Pentagon contract to a Trump Jr.-linked loan. Public grounds were converted for personal glorification, including a UFC arena on the White House lawn. Yet courts pushed back forcefully: a judge blocked a $1.776 billion patronage fund, another protected the Kennedy Center, and thirty-five former judges intervened against the fund's legal basis. The Democracy Clock held nearly still, closing at 7:42 p.m., a fraction of a minute behind where it began, because erosion and resistance moved in near lockstep.

Short Synopsis:
Federal force against protesters and a senator, a collapsed Iran deal, and spreading self-dealing were met by unusually forceful judicial pushback, including an injunction against a $1.776 billion patronage fund. The clock barely moved, reflecting a week where erosion and resistance were nearly evenly matched.
