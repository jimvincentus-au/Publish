This week centered on Washington, D.C. Citing a crime emergency contradicted by 30-year-low violent crime statistics, President Trump declared an emergency, deployed the National Guard, and seized control of the Metropolitan Police Department — then floated eliminating D.C.'s home rule altogether. By week's end, legal challenges and a negotiated agreement partially rolled back the takeover, though the president signaled he would seek permanent congressional authority. In parallel, Trump fired the Bureau of Labor Statistics commissioner after a weak jobs report and nominated a partisan loyalist to replace her, raising alarms about the integrity of federal economic data.
