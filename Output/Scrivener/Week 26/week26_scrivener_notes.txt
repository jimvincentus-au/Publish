Synopses
--------
Long Synopsis:
Week 26 recorded no net movement on the Democracy Clock, holding at 7:41 p.m.—not because nothing happened, but because what happened had already been priced into prior erosion. Immigration enforcement intensified through raids, expanded detention, and curtailed bond hearings. Ethics oversight inside the Justice Department was gutted, while Congress ratified sweeping cuts to public broadcasting and foreign aid. The Epstein files controversy fractured the governing coalition from within, as containment efforts collapsed under pressure from allies rather than opponents. Meanwhile, Texas moved to redraw congressional maps at presidential urging, tariffs shook consumer prices, and public information systems—from NASA climate data to labor statistics—grew less transparent. Courts and civil society offered real but partial resistance. The week reads less as crisis than as confirmation: coercive tools, once exceptional, now operate as routine governance.

Short Synopsis:
Despite zero clock movement, Week 26 showed consolidation across immigration enforcement, civil service politicization, and information control, while the Epstein files controversy exposed real fractures inside the governing coalition itself.
