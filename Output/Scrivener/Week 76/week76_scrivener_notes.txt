Title
-----
Executive Alignment Without Net Movement

Subtitle
--------
Courts, agencies, Congress, and public symbolism all pushed toward tighter presidential control, even as narrower rulings held back a larger clock shift.

Tagline / Hook
-------------
No single rupture defined the week; instead, executive control, coercive enforcement, and public-private fusion advanced together while courts held only partial lines.

Synopses
--------
Long Synopsis:
Week 76 showed a governing system becoming more coordinated in how it concentrates power. The Supreme Court weakened long-standing buffers around independent agencies, and the White House immediately claimed broader authority over regulators and senior officials. Immigration pressure intensified through TPS terminations, mass ICE arrests, local cooperation mandates, and new enforcement rhetoric, even after the Court preserved birthright citizenship. Election access faced renewed pressure through proof-of-citizenship demands, early-voting restrictions, and litigation framed as routine integrity policy. At the same time, financial disclosures, family-linked foreign deals, crypto deregulation, redirected public funds, and no-bid projects documented a deeper fusion of office, money, and leader display. Resistance remained real but bounded: courts protected some mail-ballot rules, digital privacy, and limited agency independence. The clock did not move because structural erosion and institutional pushback offset each other.

Short Synopsis:
The week brought stronger presidential claims over agencies, harsher immigration enforcement, tighter election pressure, and clearer evidence of self-dealing. Courts still imposed meaningful limits on birthright citizenship, mail ballots, and geofence surveillance, leaving the Democracy Clock unchanged at 7:44 p.m. despite a harder governing pattern.
