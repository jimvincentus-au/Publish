The Democracy Clock held nearly still this week, closing at 7:33 p.m., a tenth of a minute from where it began. That stillness masked intense collision. The administration escalated across many fronts simultaneously: it arrested a sitting judge for a courtroom ruling, signed nine executive orders in one day gutting civil rights enforcement, defied court orders on deportation, and pressured universities with funding cuts. Courts pushed back hard, issuing at least eleven rulings against the administration in a single week. The Supreme Court twice halted deportations under the Alien Enemies Act. Harvard refused governance demands and sued, joined by over 150 university presidents. Meanwhile, citizens and green-card holders were detained without adequate verification, and Pentagon dysfunction deepened with a second classified-information breach. Erosion spread wide rather than struck singularly, met by institutions still capable of resisting, but visibly strained by the effort required to hold the line.
