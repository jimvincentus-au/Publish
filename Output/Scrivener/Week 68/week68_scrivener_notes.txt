Title
-----
The Ruling That Moved in Days

Subtitle
--------
A single voting-rights ruling reshaped congressional maps within a week while courts and critics scrambled to keep pace with the damage.

Tagline / Hook
-------------
One ruling reordered representation in three states before the country could absorb what had happened.

Synopses
--------
Long Synopsis:
The Democracy Clock held at 7:42 p.m. this week, its stillness masking a week of intense, opposing motion. The Supreme Court's Callais ruling gutted Section 2 of the Voting Rights Act, and within days Tennessee eliminated its only majority-Black congressional district, Louisiana suspended an active primary, and Alabama's governor urged defiance of a standing federal injunction. The Justice Department pursued prosecutions widely seen as retaliatory against a former FBI director and a civil rights organization, while journalists faced leak investigations and defamation suits. An undeclared war with Iran proceeded through contradiction and reversal. Yet courts blocked sanctions against a UN expert, struck down tariffs, and preserved medication access, while civil society sued rapidly. The clock did not move because harm and resistance were, this week, evenly matched.

Short Synopsis:
A Supreme Court ruling gutting voting rights protections triggered immediate state action erasing minority representation, while an incoherent war, retaliatory prosecutions, and press intimidation continued. Courts and civil society pushed back hard enough to hold the clock steady, though not to reverse the damage already done.
