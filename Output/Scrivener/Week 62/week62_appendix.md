Week 62 Appendix: Emergency As Standing Policy
This was an acute-crisis week in which an already fragile constitutional order was stressed on multiple fronts. The Iran war and related regional escalation dominated, with the president openly threatening strikes on civilian infrastructure, deploying and surging troops without authorization, and acknowledging a “military operation” in Iran absent congressional approval. That conduct, combined with Republicans’ refusal to exercise war oversight and massive off‑books war spending, drove severe pressure on executive constraint, separation of powers, and emergency normalization. Domestically, the DHS shutdown and ICE-at-airports experiment exposed how core public services can be held hostage to immigration and voter‑suppression demands, while TSA workers and other civil servants absorbed the costs. Parallel moves to advance the SAVE Act, data‑sharing of voter rolls with DHS, and even a draft executive order to effectively nationalize and constrict voting marked a coordinated attempt to reengineer electoral access under the banner of “integrity.” Law enforcement and courts were heavily politicized: selective prosecutions, settlements for Trump allies, aggressive ballot seizures, and FBI/ICE abuses contrasted with important but reactive judicial pushback. Symbolic personalization of the state—from Trump’s signature on currency to Columbus statuary and architectural remakes—reinforced a leader‑centric, revisionist narrative amid widening global and domestic instability.
