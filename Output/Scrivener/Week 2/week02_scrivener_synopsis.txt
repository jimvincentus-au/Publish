In the first full week of the new term, the administration moved on a dozen fronts simultaneously: firing at least seventeen inspectors general without legal notice, purging DOJ prosecutors and FBI agents tied to prior investigations, and pardoning roughly 1,500 January 6 defendants including a seditious-conspiracy leader. It attempted to freeze trillions in federal funding, surged immigration enforcement with daily arrest quotas and a Guantánamo detention plan, and signed an order challenging birthright citizenship. Civil rights infrastructure fell fast, with DEI programs eliminated and transgender protections rescinded. Courts pushed back repeatedly, blocking the funding freeze and hearing challenges to the citizenship order. The public clock held nearly still, at 7:32 p.m., but the structures beneath it moved substantially, revealing a strategy built on speed itself.
