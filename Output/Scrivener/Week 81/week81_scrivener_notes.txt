Synopses
--------
Long Synopsis:
Week 81 brought no net movement on the Democracy Clock, but it clarified the architecture of erosion. The sharpest escalation came in immigration, where executive orders targeting birthright citizenship and rulings narrowing Temporary Protected Status pushed legal belonging closer to administrative discretion. ICE and DHS widened surveillance, ankle monitoring, detention capacity, and enforcement pressure, while keeping key accountability tools under agency control. At the same time, Todd Blanche’s path toward attorney general advanced through loyalty-centered bargaining, reinforcing concerns that the Justice Department is being personalized around presidential interests. Pressure also widened against journalists, public records, and independent scrutiny, while dark-money disinformation and monetized presidential communications distorted the information environment. Yet courts imposed meaningful limits in some cases, and voters in Kansas, Missouri, and local races rejected several anti-democratic moves. The week held at 7:44 p.m. because resistance slowed the advance without reversing it.

Short Synopsis:
Week 81 showed democratic pressure without net clock movement. Immigration status, surveillance, secrecy, and politicized law all worsened, but court rulings and voter resistance blocked enough additional erosion to hold the clock at 7:44 p.m.
