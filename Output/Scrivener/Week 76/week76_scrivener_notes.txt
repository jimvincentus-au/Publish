Title

The Week the Wall Came Down

Subtitle
--------
A single Supreme Court sitting handed the presidency lasting new power while resistance held just enough ground to keep the ledger nearly even.

Tagline / Hook
-------------
No tanks rolled and no election was canceled, yet by week's end the presidency held powers it did not hold before.

Synopses
--------
Long Synopsis:
Week 76 brought no dramatic rupture, yet the architecture of American governance shifted more than in almost any other week this term. The Supreme Court overruled Humphrey's Executor, granting presidents unchecked power to remove independent agency heads. The same sitting stripped protected status from hundreds of thousands of Haitians and Syrians, gutted campaign finance limits, and narrowly preserved birthright citizenship by a fragile margin already under threat. Beneath the docket, a secretive White House office quietly rebuilt federal websites outside oversight, while Trump's $2.2 billion financial disclosure revealed deep entanglement between personal wealth and public office. ICE arrests surged to record levels. Courts, individual senators, and grassroots coalitions pushed back where they could. The Democracy Clock barely moved, but the stillness concealed real and lasting consolidation.

Short Synopsis:
A single Supreme Court sitting reshaped executive power, stripped protections from immigrants, and loosened campaign finance rules, while a shadow federal office and record self-enrichment deepened the week's pattern. Courts and citizens pushed back just enough to hold the clock nearly still.
