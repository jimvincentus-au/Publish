Synopses
--------
Long Synopsis:
Week 66 was defined by expansion rather than rupture. Federal power spread across multiple systems at once: immigration raids intensified in Democratic-led cities, denaturalization plans widened the insecurity of legal status, and election administration faced new voter-challenge and data-sharing pressures. At the same time, Iran war policy mixed executive initiative with weak congressional control, contradictory public messaging, and conflict-of-interest scrutiny around Jared Kushner and related market allegations. Records fights multiplied as DOJ missed transparency deadlines, watchdogs sued for disclosure, and journalists and civil-society critics faced investigation or costly legal pressure. Inside agencies, labor protections were rolled back, the CFPB was weakened, and civil-rights enforcement was redirected. Courts and some lawmakers did provide meaningful but bounded resistance through injunctions, records orders, merger blocking, and oversight inquiries. The Democracy Clock held at 8:13 p.m., slipping only 0.1 minutes, a near-stall that masked broad institutional strain.

Short Synopsis:
Power widened through immigration enforcement, secrecy, election pressure, and agency hollowing, while courts and oversight slowed but did not reverse the week's cumulative democratic erosion.
