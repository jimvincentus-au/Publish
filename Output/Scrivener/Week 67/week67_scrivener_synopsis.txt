Week 67 brought no single democratic rupture, but several reinforcing ones. The Supreme Court weakened Section 2 of the Voting Rights Act in Louisiana v. Callais, and Republican officials in Florida, Tennessee, Alabama, Mississippi, and Louisiana quickly moved to redraw maps or disrupt election calendars under the new standard. Immigration power widened through speech-based green-card guidance, renewed family detention, harsher detention conditions, judge replacements, and major new enforcement funding. The presidency also drew public symbolism and public resources closer to the person of Donald Trump through the White House ballroom push, Trump-branded passports, and family-linked benefits. Meanwhile, the FCC increased pressure on media, and environmental agencies tilted further toward favored industries while EPA scientific capacity was cut back. Courts and committees still imposed some limits, but mostly after the fact. The clock held steady because fresh damage was offset by partial, reactive checks rather than any real reversal.
