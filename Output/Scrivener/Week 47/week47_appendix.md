# Week 47 Appendix: Hierarchy as Governance

This was a heavy-erosion week, with the executive branch aggressively consolidating power while civil society, some courts, and a few legislatures mounted notable but limited resistance. Structurally, the most intense pressure fell on checks and balances, rule-of-law equality, and the information environment. Trump’s AI preemption order, the national security strategy, and threats around tariffs and independent agencies all push toward a presidency that treats Congress, states, and regulators as subordinate. Immigration and border policy hardened into a quasi-militarized regime, with new deportation aircraft, a militarized zone in California, Afghan asylum arrests, and ICE abuses, deepening stratified citizenship and normalizing security forces as tools of political theater. Simultaneously, crony capitalism and foreign-influence risks expanded through Nvidia chip sales to China and Kushner-linked sovereign wealth financing of media consolidation. On the repair side, courts blocked some overreach (National Guard in LA, unlawful prosecutor appointment, Menendez ban, Abrego rulings), Indiana Republicans rejected a Trump-backed gerrymander, Congress tied Hegseth’s travel budget to transparency, and states like Illinois pushed back on immigration enforcement. But these defenses were outpaced by executive and structural moves that entrench unilateralism, impunity, and elite-driven policy.
