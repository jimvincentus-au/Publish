Week 67 measured almost no net movement on the Democracy Clock, but the stillness concealed sharp countervailing forces. The Supreme Court's ruling in Louisiana v. Callais gutted Section 2 of the Voting Rights Act, and within days Louisiana suspended an active primary while Tennessee, Mississippi, Alabama, and Florida moved to eliminate majority-Black districts. A shooting at the White House Correspondents' Dinner became grounds for the Justice Department to pressure a nonprofit into dropping a lawsuit, for Congress to rush ballroom funding bills, and for officials to blame a peaceful protest movement and an entire political party. James Comey faced a second indictment over an ambiguous social media post. Resistance held in narrow places: a nonprofit's refusal, one governor's restraint, dismissed lawsuits, and congressional inquiries. The week showed how fast old tools move once picked up.
