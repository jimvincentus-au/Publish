Title
-----
Midstream Rule Changes and Pressure

Subtitle
--------
Election maps, law enforcement, media regulation, and immigration power all tightened at once while courts supplied only partial resistance.

Tagline / Hook
-------------
The clock did not move, but power spread through more channels at once, making democratic erosion broader, harder to track, and harder to resist.

Synopses
--------
Long Synopsis:
Week 68 brought no single rupture, but a broad reinforcement of anti-democratic patterns across many institutions at once. After a Supreme Court ruling weakened Voting Rights Act protections, Republican officials rapidly reopened redistricting fights, with Tennessee dismantling its only majority-Black congressional district and other states moving to redraw maps mid-cycle. Federal law enforcement and prosecutors increased pressure on critics, election workers, and political actors, while the Justice Department also sought to shield the president in a personal case. Regulators and investigators leaned on broadcasters, reporters, and news organizations through license reviews, leak probes, and lawsuits. Immigration enforcement intensified despite court limits, with re-arrests, deadly operations, harms to children, and long-term funding expansion. Abroad, war powers remained concentrated in the executive as military action, naming, costs, and evidence were managed through secrecy. The clock held steady because these were consolidations, not a fresh leap.

Short Synopsis:
The week deepened existing patterns rather than creating a new break: mid-cycle election changes, selective law enforcement, pressure on the press, harsher immigration enforcement, and executive secrecy around war. Courts still imposed some friction, but the dominant movement was consolidation.
