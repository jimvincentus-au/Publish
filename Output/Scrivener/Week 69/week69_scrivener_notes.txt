Title
-----
Procedural Power, Partisan Ends

Subtitle
--------
Across elections, immigration, regulation, and public records, formal authority was used less neutrally and more as a tool of control.

Tagline / Hook
-------------
The clock did not move because the system was calm; it held because familiar abuses spread through ordinary channels with greater confidence.

Synopses
--------
Long Synopsis:
Week 69 brought no single rupture, but a broad hardening of anti-democratic practice across ordinary institutions. The Democracy Clock held at 8:13 p.m., not because pressure eased, but because familiar patterns deepened rather than breaking into a wholly new phase. After weakened voting-rights protections, courts and state officials moved quickly to redraw maps, dilute Black representation, and even halt an election already underway in Louisiana. Immigration enforcement grew harsher and less visible through accelerated removals, offshore detention, and secrecy around deportation flights. At the same time, political pressure reached into health and environmental agencies, while no-bid contracting and leader-serving spending blurred public purpose and private advantage. Congress, courts, cities, and watchdog groups still resisted in places, but mostly from a defensive posture. The week showed democratic forms still operating while power learned to use them for punishment, concealment, and partisan gain.

Short Synopsis:
The week’s central pattern was consolidation through procedure: election rules, immigration law, regulation, contracting, and records systems were used to reward allies, punish critics, and narrow accountability, even as courts and local institutions mounted partial resistance.
