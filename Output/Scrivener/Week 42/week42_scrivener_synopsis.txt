Week 42 concentrated an unusual density of confrontation into seven days. SNAP benefits lapsed for the first time in sixty-one years, and the administration resisted a court order to fund them, with the vice president calling the ruling absurd before the Supreme Court paused relief. The Justice Department indicted Comey, James, and Bolton within days of a presidential demand, while a federal magistrate rebuked the process and a Reagan-appointed judge resigned in protest. Decisive Democratic wins in Virginia, New Jersey, New York City, and California's redistricting vote met fraud claims, election monitors, and a Fourteenth Amendment gambit against New York's mayor-elect. Courts blocked National Guard deployments in five cities and issued a permanent injunction in Portland, even as unilateral war-making abroad continued unchecked. A mass pardon of 2020 election-subversion figures closed the week. The clock moved barely at all, reflecting rough balance between severe pressure and real resistance.
