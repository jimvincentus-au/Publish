Title
-----
Speed as a Method of Rule

Subtitle
--------
In its first full week back, the new administration opened a dozen fronts at once, testing whether pace alone could outrun courts, Congress, and the press.

Tagline / Hook
-------------
When a government moves on every front at once, the question isn't which battle to fight. It's whether anyone can fight them all in time.

Synopses
--------
Long Synopsis:
In the first full week of the new term, the administration moved on a dozen fronts simultaneously: firing at least seventeen inspectors general without legal notice, purging DOJ prosecutors and FBI agents tied to prior investigations, and pardoning roughly 1,500 January 6 defendants including a seditious-conspiracy leader. It attempted to freeze trillions in federal funding, surged immigration enforcement with daily arrest quotas and a Guantánamo detention plan, and signed an order challenging birthright citizenship. Civil rights infrastructure fell fast, with DEI programs eliminated and transgender protections rescinded. Courts pushed back repeatedly, blocking the funding freeze and hearing challenges to the citizenship order. The public clock held nearly still, at 7:32 p.m., but the structures beneath it moved substantially, revealing a strategy built on speed itself.

Short Synopsis:
The administration's first full week opened a dozen fronts at once: watchdog firings, prosecutor purges, mass pardons, a funding freeze, an immigration surge, and an attack on birthright citizenship. Courts held their ground. The clock barely moved, but the architecture beneath it shifted substantially.

Actionable Outcomes
-------------------
- Contact congressional representatives to demand hearings on the legality of the inspector general firings under the 30-day notice statute.
- Support organizations providing legal aid to immigrants affected by expanded ICE enforcement, including know-your-rights education.
- Monitor and support the multistate lawsuits challenging the birthright citizenship executive order.
- Follow and support litigation challenging the federal funding freeze to protect congressionally appropriated programs in your state.
- Contact state attorneys general to encourage continued legal scrutiny of executive overreach on funding and immigration.
- Support civil service employee associations and unions resisting reclassification of federal workers as at-will employees.
- Document and report irregularities in immigration enforcement, including wrongful detentions of citizens, to civil rights organizations.
