Synopses
--------
Long Synopsis:
Week 80 showed democratic erosion working through routine channels rather than spectacle. The administration pressed a coordinated campaign against the Smithsonian, using executive orders, White House messaging, hearings, and reported appointment delays to shape national memory. At the same time, House Republicans and the White House advanced mail-voting and voter-ID restrictions through legislation, Senate pressure, and Supreme Court appeals. Immigration enforcement hardened after TPS protections were lifted, with airport arrests, detention abuses, and child-welfare information feeding deportation pipelines. Executive reach also widened through partisan grant cuts, unilateral tariffs, and expansive war-powers claims, while press access and public disclosure narrowed around diplomacy and conflict. Courts blocked some actions and the Senate delayed Todd Blanche's attorney general vote, but these checks were mostly reactive. The clock stayed at 7:44 p.m., moving only 0.1 minutes, reflecting consolidation more than calm.

Short Synopsis:
Week 80 brought little spectacle but steady consolidation: the White House pressured the Smithsonian, voting restrictions advanced, immigration coercion widened, and executive discretion expanded. Courts and some senators slowed parts of the agenda, but mostly from a defensive posture. The Democracy Clock remained at 7:44 p.m., edging forward by 0.1 minutes.
