Week 12 opened with a self-inflicted trade shock: sweeping tariffs imposed by emergency memo, reversed within days after markets lost trillions. That volatility framed a deeper story. The administration punished a DOJ attorney for telling a court the truth, slow-walked a unanimous Supreme Court order to return a wrongfully deported man, stripped law firms of security clearances for representing adversaries, and froze billions in university funding to compel ideological compliance. Judges faced impeachment calls and disqualification attempts. Yet resistance matched the pressure: Harvard refused federal control, the American Bar Association condemned intimidation, courts reinstated fired officials and restored press access, and millions marched peacefully across thirteen hundred cities. The Democracy Clock moved only six-hundredths of a minute, a stillness reflecting near-equal force pushing in opposite directions rather than an absence of consequence.
