Week 68 did not produce a dramatic clock jump, but it deepened an existing pattern of democratic hardening. After the Supreme Court weakened Voting Rights Act protections, Republican officials in several states moved quickly to redraw maps, alter election timing, and dilute minority representation, with Tennessee offering the clearest example. At the federal level, executive power pressed into election administration through mail-voting controls and demands for voter and poll-worker data. Immigration enforcement escalated through re-arrests after judicial release, court closures, force against civilians and children, and plans for long-term capacity growth. Meanwhile, legal and regulatory tools were used against critics, journalists, and advocacy groups, while war information around Iran was narrowed through secrecy, disputed cost reporting, and claims that conflict had ended while exchanges continued. Courts imposed some limits, but not enough to change the week's balance. The clock held at 8:13 p.m.
