Donald Trump's second term opened not with a transition but with a flood: dozens of executive orders signed on inauguration day alone, followed by a week of proclamations, memoranda, and personnel purges that touched nearly every corner of federal governance. The administration moved to end birthright citizenship, close the border to asylum seekers, erase federal recognition of transgender Americans, dismantle diversity programs, and pardon roughly 1,500 people convicted in the January 6 Capitol attack. It declared national emergencies at the border and over energy, invoked authority to bypass a congressionally passed law banning TikTok, and fired inspectors general across a dozen agencies
