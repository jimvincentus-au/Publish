Week 72 Appendix: Administrative Hardening Without Advance
This was a high-pressure week defined by politicization of state machinery across multiple fronts at once: the civil service, the justice system, immigration enforcement, intelligence appointments, and information control. The strongest structural signal came from efforts to convert neutral institutions into loyalty-based instruments—most clearly through the Schedule F-style order stripping protections from senior federal workers, the appointment of unqualified loyalists and January 6 figures to sensitive posts, and continuing DOJ actions that look retaliatory toward Trump critics while preserving special protections for Trump and his family. Immigration was the other major pressure point: abusive detention conditions, reduced death-reporting transparency, asset-seizure threats, and a massive Senate enforcement package all deepened the sense of a coercive apparatus expanding faster than oversight. Courts provided meaningful but partial counterpressure—blocking some executive actions, protecting asylum processing, and checking anti-trans and institutional overreach—but these repairs were offset by the Supreme Court’s Alabama map ruling and by the administration’s increasingly maximal claims of executive authority. Overall, the week showed democratic erosion less as a single rupture than as coordinated institutional conversion.
